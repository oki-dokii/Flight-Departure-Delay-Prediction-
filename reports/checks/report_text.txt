1 Problem and checkpoints
The target is LATE = 1 when gate departure occurs at least 15 minutes after its scheduled gate departure.
Takeoff delay is a different outcome. We predict at 360 minutes (six hours) and 60 minutes (one hour) before
scheduled UTC gate departure. Every input must be available at or before that flight’s checkpoint; ambiguous
availability is blocked. Both horizons use the same eligible flights still awaiting departure at the one-hour
checkpoint. The repository is https://github.com/oki-dokii/Flight-Departure-Delay-Prediction- .

3 Cleaning and UTC handling
F ull-development validation (audit). P1 owns cleaning and UTC facts. The documented pipeline
removes exact duplicates, cancellations or unknown cancellation status, unmatched origin zones and unusable
departure fields; it retains diverted flights with usable departures and preserves missing tails explicitly.
It interprets BTS local FlightDate and HHMM using the origin zone, treating 2400 as next midnight.
TimezoneFinder 6.5.9 and tzdata 2025.2 are pinned. DepDelay supplies elapsed time to reconstruct actual
departure and the published actual local clock is checked separately. Equality of reconstructed UTC delay
with DepDelay is partly by construction, not independent validation.
A-SCHED assumes schedule timestamps are correct and known at T. A computation over development
local scheduled clocks finds zero ambiguous fall-back rows; the conservative union of US-origin timezone
ambiguity sets is empty. Thus the outcome-assisted branch cannot choose between distinct schedule instants
for these development rows. The assumption does not validate retrospective schedule versioning or the
closed test window: local 01:00–01:59 on November 2 can have two readings in DST-observing origins, and
the cleaner excludes ambiguous scheduled wall clocks before validating the actual clock.
The committed arrival checks record 153 scheduled-arrival clock mismatches, 79 actual-arrival mismatches
and 16,783 actual arrivals unavailable for comparison. P1’s Phase B copy masks mismatched timestamps;
that masking is not present in P2’s analyzed Phase A file. No raw ZIPs or test-period flight records were
opened.
4 Split design and evaluation
Four expanding training windows validate on July, August, September and October. Both scheduled and
observed training departures must precede the cutoff one day before validation begins. All selection uses
get_folds() positional indices with .iloc. Preprocessing is fitted within the training draw. Development
uses scheduled UTC times in [2025-01-01, 2025-10-31); the following 48 hours are excluded. The closed test
begins 2025-11-02 00:00 UTC . Its count and metrics are pending P1’s single final run after freeze.
Seeds 42, 73 and 101 vary common month-stratified training draws (about 90% of available training
flights, capped at about 500,000). Validation retains all eligible rows in the declared cohort. PR-AUC
means average precision and is the selection metric; ROC-AUC is reported alongside it. The harness reports
population standard deviation (ddof=0) across four folds times three seeds. That pooled spread mostly
reflects fold drift, so P2 also exports within-fold between-seed spread. Pilot and full-population results are
kept explicitly separate.

6 Feature engineering
6.1 Prediction Checkpoints and Full-Scale Feature Architecture
The departure delay prediction task is framed as a binary classification problem: predicting whether a
scheduled commercial domestic flight will depart its origin gate with a delay ≥ 15 minutes (LATE = 1 ) or
< 15 minutes (LATE = 0), based on reconstructed UTC gate departure timestamps (OBSER VED − TIME).
Features are computed strictly relative to two pre-departure prediction checkpoints:
1. Strategic Dispatch Horizon ( T = 360 min / 6h): Models early-day dispatch planning and baseline
weather regimes before surface queues build.
2. T actical Gate Horizon ( T = 60 min / 1h): Models gate operations, recent surface weather obser-
vations, 3-hour origin departure congestion, and inbound aircraft arrival status.
The feature matrices retain the original development row order. The cutoff check covered all 5,749,041
flights at each horizon. It found one flight whose own departure had to be excluded from its one-hour
congestion history. That flight also fails the common target eligibility rule because it had already departed.
6.2 Anti-Leakage Safeguards and Weather Integration
The feature pipeline ( src/features.py and src/weather.py) enforces four protocol guarantees:
• Strict Cutoff Rule: No event timestamped after cutoff T = TIME − ∆T enters computation.
• T arget Self-Exclusion: Origin airport 3-hour congestion ( origin_departures_3h,
origin_late_rate_3h) excludes the target flight itself when present in the history pool, even if it
pushed back early. Cancellation rates are excluded because BTS records do not timestamp when
cancellations were announced.
• Hourly Surface W eather Join (Decision D2): Routine METAR and SPECI observations
(3,826,099 records covering 347/349 origin airports, Jan 2025–Jan 2026) were retrieved from the
public-domain Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM) ASOS archive and joined via strict backward
merge_asof (obs_time_utc ≤ cutoffT ). Observations older than 6h or from unmapped stations
trigger weather_missing = 1 without silent imputation.
• Decision D1 Quarantine (T ail-Chain F eatures): Because BTS reports retrospective final tail
numbers rather than live dispatch assignments, inbound aircraft features are excluded from the
primary set ( G1) and isolated in G3 as an ablation under the operational-feed assumption.

the aircraft assignment at the checkpoint. To quantify how much predictive signal each information source

We sampled eligible targets without replacement, proportionally by UTC month, from January through
October with seed 42. The July through October folds used only earlier flights for fitting. Both checkpoints
use the same targets and retain congestion histories from the full source. Each cell gives the mean and
population standard deviation across four folds and three training seeds. The table covers 240 validation
outputs. LightGBM led at both checkpoints. All nine non-baseline configurations improved in mean average
precision at one hour. Month-proportional sampling made repeated fits feasible while retaining the monthly
traﬀic mix. We sampled prediction targets once and kept their feature values from the published full-
development feature release. For each of the 200,000 IDs at each horizon, both the three-hour departure
count and the late fraction matched the source matrix exactly. Mean departure counts in the full and
sampled matrices were 37.986 and 38.054 at six hours, and 53.558 and 53.637 at one hour.
The validation months contain 21,430, 20,762, 19,478 and 20,353 targets, respectively. During tuning,
seeds vary training draws and may vary the estimator. Selected full-fold refits use the same eligible training
targets for each seed within a fold. The reported spread measures variation conditional on one cohort draw.
For selected LightGBM, the three seeds gave identical AP to reported precision within each month. Its
AP spread therefore reflects differences between months. Sensitivity to drawing a different cohort remains
unmeasured.

11 Limitations
F ull-development validation (population audit); Pilot (PSI and calibration); Held-out test
(closed). Reporting-carrier domestic coverage limits transfer to other operators and international flights.
Excluding cancellations and unusable departures conditions the target and undercounts congestion. January–
October of one year cannot characterize recurring seasonality or the closed November–December regime.
Carrier/airport effects are confounded by route mix and shared shocks.
BTS monthly reporting is retrospective, not a live feed. A-SCHED and A-HISTORY are explicit opera-
tional interpretations, not evidence of schedule versioning or latency. D1 excludes unverified tail assignments
from primary inputs; the G3 operational-feed and continuity assumptions remain pending approval. The
cleaner excludes ambiguous local scheduled times during the fall-back hour. Arrival clocks have 153 sched-
uled mismatches, 79 actual mismatches and 16,783 actual arrivals not comparable; P2’s Phase A file is
unmasked and no arrival predictor is used. Weather unit conversions and public-domain terms have been
reviewed, but PBI/PPG station identity, partial-null/zero-fill semantics and receipt/revision timing remain
unresolved. The loader reads a mixed-period weather artifact; P2 did not run it. The published matrices
use the canonical weather indicator.
Full G0 and G1 feature drift, G0 tree and logistic calibration, and descriptive weather and inbound
analyses use development flights. The selected boosting calibration and the month ablation use the paired
pilot. Fold variation and repeated validation flights limit the interpretation of pooled seed spreads. Final
test starts 2025-11-02 UTC; its flight count and results remain pending P1’s single final run.
